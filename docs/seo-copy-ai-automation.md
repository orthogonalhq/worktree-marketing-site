### Section: operational friction

**Eyebrow:** Work that keeps asking for attention

## The task is familiar. Reassembling it every time is the problem.

Many operational bottlenecks are not one difficult task. They are the same context gathering, follow-up, re-entry, preparation, and handoff repeated across a week. The work moves only when someone remembers what to check, where to look, and who needs to act next.

AI workflow automation services can create useful capacity when that routine is understood as a complete path—not reduced to one model response or isolated integration.

**Recognizable signals:**

- The same information is collected from several places for every case.
- A handoff waits because ownership or the next action is unclear.
- A team member repeatedly prepares the same kind of update, brief, or packet.
- Follow-up depends on one person remembering what changed.
- Exceptions disappear into inboxes, chats, or private notes.
- The process has a known rhythm but requires constant coordination to maintain it.

**Display direction:** Use calm operational artifacts: an incomplete handoff, a status request, a duplicated update, and an exception waiting for an owner.

### Section: workflow fit

<a id="workflow-fit"></a>

**Eyebrow:** Choose the work before the technology

## Start where the work already has a rhythm.

A strong first workflow does not need to be simple. It does need to be understandable. Your team should be able to describe what begins the work, which information matters, what usually happens, where judgment belongs, and how it knows the work is complete.

**A useful starting point usually has:**

- A recurring event or request.
- A process owner and people who know the current routine.
- Identifiable inputs and systems.
- A repeatable path with known exceptions.
- Decisions that can be separated from preparation and coordination.
- A practical way to judge whether the result is useful.

When the process itself is unclear, the first improvement may be eliminating a step, choosing a source of truth, or agreeing on ownership. Worktree helps make those decisions before automating them.

### Section: improve before automating

**Eyebrow:** Better work before more technology

## Remove what should not be repeated.

Automation is most useful after the team has challenged the routine itself. A duplicate approval, conflicting status field, unnecessary report, or unclear handoff should not become faster simply because a system can perform it.

Worktree works through the operating path with your team:

1. **Eliminate** work that no longer needs to exist.
2. **Simplify** unnecessary steps and handoffs.
3. **Clarify** ownership, source information, and expected results.
4. **Automate** the repeatable work that remains.
5. **Keep people involved** where judgment, authority, or an unusual case requires them.

The result may use an agent, deterministic automation, system integration, or a combination. The technology follows the job.

### Section: managed workflow anatomy

**Eyebrow:** A complete operating path

## Give every case a clear next step.

Worktree designs the automation around the whole routine so the normal work can advance and the unusual work can reach a person.

#### Signal

A request, schedule, message, or selected system event starts the routine.

#### Context

The workflow gathers the permitted information needed for the case.

#### Action

It follows the agreed instructions to prepare or perform the next step.

#### Judgment

An approval, exception, or ambiguous case reaches the right person with useful context.

#### Output

The result is placed where the team expects it, with the relevant operating evidence available for review.

### Section: frequently asked questions

**Eyebrow:** Before the workflow review

## Common automation questions.

#### What are AI automation services?

For Worktree, AI automation services begin with a recurring business workflow and continue through process design, implementation, testing, launch, and managed operation. The goal is useful operational capacity, not automation for its own sake.

#### Do we need to know whether the workflow requires an AI agent?

No. Bring the work as it happens today. Worktree assesses the routine, systems, decisions, and exceptions before recommending an agent, deterministic automation, integration, process change, or combination.

#### What kinds of workflows are a good starting point?

Look for work that recurs, has an owner, uses identifiable information, follows a known path, and has a practical standard for a useful result. The workflow may include exceptions and human decisions as long as those can be made explicit.

#### Will automation remove people from the process?

Not where their judgment or authority is needed. Worktree designs the workflow so routine preparation and coordination can advance while approvals, ambiguity, and exceptions reach the appropriate person.

#### Can Worktree use our existing systems?

The workflow review identifies the selected systems, access, actions, and technical dependencies. Worktree confirms feasibility and the integration approach before committing to scope.

#### How is pricing determined?

The workflow review clarifies the process, systems, permissions, evaluation requirements, operating support, and other dependencies. Worktree uses that understanding to define the scope and commercial proposal.
