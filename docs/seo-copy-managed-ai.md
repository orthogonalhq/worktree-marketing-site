### Section: after launch

**Eyebrow:** Production changes the work

## Launch creates an operating responsibility.

A workflow that passed its test cases can still encounter new language, changed policies, missing information, altered system behavior, and exceptions no one predicted. The business needs a way to notice those signals, decide what matters, and make changes without losing the original controls.

Managed AI operations give that responsibility a home. Worktree stays close to the deployed workflow and the people who own its result so operating evidence can become a focused decision about what happens next.

**Supporting points:**

- Quality is compared with the accepted standard.
- Exceptions and failures have a path to follow.
- Material changes are tested and recorded.
- Team questions and operating issues receive support during the agreed business-hours window.
- Limitations, risks, and next actions remain visible.

### Section: operating loop

<a id="operating-loop"></a>

**Eyebrow:** Evidence becomes improvement

## Review what happened. Decide what changes.

Worktree manages the workflow through a repeatable operating loop rather than a stream of untracked prompt edits.

#### 01 — Observe

Use the operating evidence established for the deployment to understand completed work, exceptions, failures, approvals, and other relevant signals.

#### 02 — Evaluate

Compare representative and live evidence with the accepted success measures and control boundaries.

#### 03 — Respond

Follow up on observed failures, exceptions, team questions, and operational issues within the agreed support model.

#### 04 — Improve

Test focused changes when evidence shows that the instructions, workflow, integration, or evaluation standard needs attention.

#### 05 — Record

Update the Deployment Record when material instructions, controls, evaluations, limitations, risks, or next actions change.

#### 06 — Review

Use the current record to decide what is working, what remains true, and what should happen next.

**Display direction:** Show the loop as an operating workspace with evidence moving into a decision and a documented change. Do not suggest that every run receives continuous human observation.

### Section: evaluation and exceptions

**Eyebrow:** Quality needs a reference point

## Judge the workflow against an agreed standard.

AI agent evaluation begins before launch, when the team defines representative cases, expected behavior, approval requirements, and acceptance criteria. After launch, those decisions provide a reference point for reviewing real operation and proposed changes.

When a failure or unusual case appears, Worktree can compare it with the accepted behavior, identify whether the issue belongs to missing context, instructions, integration, authority, or the process itself, and test a focused response.

AI agent monitoring does not mean promising that a person watches every run. It means establishing the relevant operating signals for the agreed workflow and using them within the defined support and review process.

### Section: Deployment Record

**Eyebrow:** A current account of the deployment

## Keep material changes and next actions visible.

Beginning at acceptance, the Deployment Record captures the current role, instructions, controls, evaluation standard, material changes, limitations, risks, and next actions for the managed workflow.

It helps the customer and Worktree answer practical operating questions:

- What changed since acceptance or the last review?
- Which controls and assumptions remain current?
- What did evaluation or real operation reveal?
- Which limitations or risks need attention?
- What is the next agreed action?

The Record is updated monthly and when the supported process calls for a material change to be recorded. It is an operating decision aid, not a promise that every system event is retained indefinitely.

### Section: frequently asked questions

**Eyebrow:** Before the managed-deployment review

## Common operating questions.

#### What are managed AI services?

Worktree's managed AI services cover the ongoing operation around one defined AI workflow: reviewing relevant evidence, evaluating performance, following up on failures and exceptions, supporting the team during the agreed business-hours window, testing focused changes, and maintaining the Deployment Record.

#### Is this the same as MLOps or managed cloud infrastructure?

No. Worktree manages the business workflow and its operating responsibilities. A deployment may rely on models, runtimes, and infrastructure, but generic hosting and cloud administration are not the service being described here.

#### Does Worktree monitor every run around the clock?

No. The deployment establishes the relevant operating evidence, review process, and agreed business-hours support window. Worktree does not imply continuous human observation or 24/7 service unless a separately assessed scope explicitly establishes it.

#### Who approves business decisions?

The customer retains final business authority. Worktree configures the agreed approval and escalation paths so consequential or ambiguous actions can reach the appropriate person.

#### Can Worktree manage a workflow another team built?

Potentially. Worktree first needs to assess the workflow, systems, permissions, controls, evaluation standard, operating history, and technical dependencies. The review determines whether the workflow can enter Worktree's managed service and what transition work is required.

#### How is pricing determined?

The deployment review clarifies the workflow, systems, permissions, evaluation requirements, existing implementation, support needs, and other dependencies. Worktree uses that understanding to define the scope and commercial proposal.
