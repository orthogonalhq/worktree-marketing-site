# Worktree commercial SEO page briefs

Status: Approved Phase 4 briefs  
Last updated: 2026-08-21

## Purpose

This document turns the approved query-to-URL architecture into copy-ready briefs for Worktree's first five commercial acquisition pages. It defines what each page must accomplish, what evidence it may use, where its responsibility ends, and how it should move a qualified buyer toward a deployment review.

These are briefs, not final copy or implementation authorization. They govern the next copy phase after approval.

## Governing sources

Use these sources in order when drafting:

1. `BRANDING.md` for the offer, service-versus-product posture, voice, and visual proof language.
2. `docs/seo-market-strategy.md` for the buyer, category, geographic model, and ranking objective.

If two sources appear to conflict, do not resolve the conflict in copy. Record it as a decision or evidence dependency.

## Shared commercial premise

The five pages should express one coherent offer:

> Worktree helps an established business choose a recurring workflow worth improving, implement it with explicit system and human boundaries, put it into operation, and remain involved after launch.

The service is the offer. Product surfaces such as the Consultation Blueprint, agent workflow, approvals, evaluations, run history, and Deployment Record are evidence that the service is controlled and repeatable.

The initial buyer is an operations, functional, technical, or executive leader at an established mid-market company. Operational fit matters more than a rigid employee or revenue threshold. The buyer should have recurring work, identifiable process ownership, multiple systems or handoffs, and enough business value to justify hands-on implementation and ongoing operation.

The following facts are currently supported by the approved strategy or implemented public pages and may inform the briefs:

- Worktree is operated by Orthogonal Labs Inc., based in British Columbia, Canada.

## Brief 1: AI implementation services

### Search and page role

- **Target URL:** `/services/ai-implementation`
- **Primary query:** `AI implementation services`
- **Supporting queries:** `AI agent implementation services`, `AI agent deployment services`, `AI integration services`, `AI implementation company`, `enterprise AI implementation services`, and contextual use of `AI implementation partner`
- **Search intent:** A buyer is evaluating providers that can turn an AI opportunity, workflow, or pilot into controlled production.
- **Page job:** Make Worktree's implementation competence and responsibility legible from operational discovery through acceptance and launch.

### Buyer and desired change

- **Primary buyer question:** Can this team take our real workflow into production without leaving us to coordinate strategy, integration, controls, testing, and launch ourselves?
- **Buyer context:** The buyer has an executive mandate, workflow candidate, pilot, or operating problem. They may have technical resources, but ownership is fragmented or production risk is unclear.
- **Desired operational change:** One valuable workflow moves from an informal or experimental state into a defined, tested, accepted operating routine with known systems, authority, exceptions, and owners.
- **Core proposition:** Worktree combines workflow understanding, hands-on engineering, and an explicit acceptance process so the buyer acquires a working capability rather than a prototype or recommendations deck.

### Required page argument

1. **Outcome:** Put one valuable AI-enabled workflow into operation with its responsibilities and limits understood.
2. **Why implementation fails:** The hard part is aligning the real process, source information, system access, authority, exceptions, evaluation, and ownership—not merely selecting a model.
3. **What Worktree does:** Map the work, design the operating boundaries, connect selected systems, configure the workflow, test representative and failure cases, establish acceptance, launch, and hand off into managed operation.
4. **What the customer contributes:** A process owner, people who perform the work, current guidance and examples, access to selected systems, and decisions about authority and success.
5. **What makes progress visible:** Consultation Blueprint, deployment plan, representative cases, acceptance criteria, launch checklist, and the initial Deployment Record.
6. **How integration fits:** Integration is one implementation responsibility. Explain triggers, context sources, selected tools, actions, permissions, and error paths without publishing an unsupported connector catalogue.
7. **What happens after launch:** Summarize managed operation and link to `/services/managed-ai`; do not duplicate that page.
8. **Conversion:** Invite the buyer to bring the workflow, its owner, systems, and a recent example to a deployment review.

### Suggested section sequence

1. Hero: operational outcome and hands-on implementation relationship.
2. The production gap: what sits between a promising AI idea and dependable work.
3. A recognizable workflow anatomy: trigger, context, routine, human judgment, output, and record.
4. Worktree's implementation path: understand, design, connect, test, accept, and launch.
5. Integration and control boundaries: selected systems, scoped access, approvals, exceptions, and recovery requirements where verified.
6. Tangible implementation artifacts: Blueprint, test cases, acceptance criteria, launch checklist, and initial Record.
7. Best-fit engagement signals and what the customer brings.
8. Managed-after-launch bridge.
9. Deployment-review CTA.

### Evidence and visuals

- Use a workflow map, systems-and-permissions matrix, representative test set, approval policy, and launch checklist as designed artifacts.
- Show one illustrative path through systems, with a human decision visibly retained.
- Prefer a labeled demonstration over an invented case study.
- Link factual control detail to `/product/security` and product-system detail to `/product`.

### Commercial and accuracy boundaries

- Do not promise a universal implementation timeline.
- Do not imply every process is suitable for an agent.
- Do not claim compatibility with named systems until verified.
- Do not imply Worktree replaces the buyer's process owner, legal authority, or policy decisions.
- Do not turn `enterprise` into unsupported procurement, compliance, scale, or SLA claims.
- Keep strategy subordinate to implementation; Worktree is not selling an open-ended transformation program.

### Conversion and links

- **Primary CTA:** `Start an implementation review` to `/deploy`.
- **CTA preparation:** Ask the reader to bring the current workflow, owner, systems involved, recent examples, and the change they want.
- **Required links:** `/services/ai-automation`, `/services/managed-ai`, `/product/security`, `/use-cases`, `/product`, and later `/blog/ai-automation-cost`.
- **Provisional metadata direction:** Title should lead with `AI Implementation Services`; description should name workflow mapping, integration, controls, testing, launch, and ongoing accountability without keyword stacking.

### Brief acceptance test

An unfamiliar buyer should understand what Worktree implements, why implementation is more than model selection, which responsibilities Worktree assumes, what the customer must provide, what artifacts prove progress, and what happens after launch.

## Brief 2: AI automation services

### Search and page role

- **Target URL:** `/services/ai-automation`
- **Primary query:** `AI automation services`
- **Supporting queries:** `AI workflow automation services`, `business automation services`, `business workflow automation`, `AI business process automation`, and limited comparison use of `AI automation agency`
- **Search intent:** A buyer recognizes recurring manual work and is evaluating outside help, but may not know whether the answer is an agent, deterministic automation, integration, or process redesign.
- **Page job:** Help a qualified buyer identify a worthwhile workflow and understand Worktree's workflow-first automation approach.

### Buyer and desired change

- **Primary buyer question:** Can you find and improve the work that keeps consuming capacity without automating the wrong process or removing necessary judgment?
- **Buyer context:** The operation contains recurring re-entry, follow-up, status gathering, document preparation, handoffs, or exceptions. The buyer sees the cost but may not have a clean specification.
- **Desired operational change:** A repeatable workflow advances with less manual coordination, while necessary decisions, exceptions, and accountability remain visible to people.
- **Core proposition:** Worktree starts with the work as it actually happens, then chooses and implements the appropriate combination of workflow design, automation, integration, and agent behavior.

### Required page argument

1. **Recognition:** Show recurring operational friction in language an operations buyer recognizes.
2. **Selection:** Explain what makes a strong first workflow: frequency, a describable playbook, identifiable inputs, measurable success, and clear authority.
3. **Restraint:** Some work should be eliminated or simplified before automation; some decisions should remain human.
4. **Workflow design:** Define the event, context, systems, routine, approval, exception, output, owner, and success measure.
5. **Delivery bridge:** Once the workflow is selected, Worktree implements and launches it; link to the implementation owner for detail.
6. **Operating bridge:** The workflow must continue working as systems and real cases change; link to managed AI.
7. **Examples:** Use a small set of distinct illustrative patterns, not a catalogue of industry pages or fictional results.
8. **Conversion:** Invite review of one recurring workflow rather than a broad automation transformation.

### Suggested section sequence

1. Hero: create reliable capacity around recurring work.
2. Operational symptoms: follow-ups slip, context is reassembled, handoffs wait, updates are re-entered, exceptions hide.
3. Choose the right first workflow: fit and non-fit signals.
4. Improve before automating: eliminate, simplify, standardize, then automate where useful.
5. Anatomy of a managed workflow.
6. Illustrative workflow patterns with explicit demonstration labels.
7. From workflow review to implementation and managed operation.
8. Control and human-judgment summary with security link.
9. Workflow-review CTA.

### Evidence and visuals

- Use before-state artifacts such as an overloaded handoff, repeated status chase, or scattered context—not fabricated performance metrics.
- Show how an illustrative routine moves from signal to recorded output.
- Make the approval or exception point visually prominent.
- Draw supporting insight from the existing workflow-redesign article without treating IBM's evidence as Worktree customer proof.

### Commercial and accuracy boundaries

- Do not promise headcount replacement, universal time savings, or ROI.
- Do not describe Worktree as a no-code automation shop or generic agency.
- Do not imply all recurring work should be automated.
- Do not list industries, systems, or outcomes without evidence.
- Keep the implementation method summarized; the implementation page owns the full production path.
- Use `AI automation agency` only to clarify the buyer's choice, not as Worktree's identity.

### Conversion and links

- **Primary CTA:** `Review a workflow` to `/deploy`.
- **CTA preparation:** Ask for one recurring workflow, its frequency, systems, owner, current examples, and reason it matters.
- **Required links:** `/services/ai-implementation`, `/services/managed-ai`, `/use-cases`, `/product/security`, `/product/agents`, and later `/blog/ai-automation-cost`.
- **Provisional metadata direction:** Title should lead with `AI Automation Services`; description should qualify the offer around recurring business workflows, system context, human approvals, and managed implementation.

### Brief acceptance test

The reader should recognize a suitable operational problem, understand why Worktree begins with workflow design rather than a favored tool, and know that implementation and ongoing operation are available without mistaking the page for a generic automation catalogue.

3. The managed operating loop: observe, evaluate, respond, change, record, review.

6. Deployment Record and monthly accountability.
7. Support boundaries and change scope.
8. Product-proof bridge to managed-operations surfaces.

- Use an illustrative incident or changed-system scenario without promising response times.

### Commercial and accuracy boundaries

- Do not adopt generic MLOps, model-hosting, cloud-infrastructure, or cybersecurity positioning.

- Every commercial page has one buyer question, one desired operational change, and one primary query owner.
