# Worktree SEO query priorities

Status: Approved research baseline  
Last updated: 2026-08-20

## Purpose

This document converts the search territory in `docs/seo-market-strategy.md` into a prioritized query set. It is the Phase 2 deliverable defined by `docs/seo-execution-plan.md` and should govern the keyword-to-URL architecture that follows.

The research asks one question: which search intents are most likely to bring Worktree established mid-market buyers who want a real AI-enabled workflow implemented and kept operating?

## Ratification

The evidence, limitations, query clusters, and page-level conclusions in this document were approved on 2026-08-20.

This approval establishes:

- The public, no-credential dataset as Worktree's directional demand baseline until authenticated Google Ads, paid SEO-platform, or Search Console data supersedes it.
- `AI implementation services`, `AI automation services`, and `managed AI services` as the three page-owning national commercial clusters.
- `AI implementation partner` as homepage and category language rather than a standalone page requirement.
- Agent implementation, agent deployment, workflow automation, and managed operations as high-fit refinements within the broader measurable clusters.
- `AI automation Vancouver` as the primary validated local commercial phrase, supported by implementation and consulting language.
- The adjacent-query set below as an approved research queue, not as authorization to create additional pages before Phase 3 resolves intent overlap and URL ownership.

## Research boundary

The findings combine a qualitative review of current United States, Canadian, and Vancouver search results with public, no-credential demand estimates collected on 2026-08-20. The review assessed result composition, dominant page formats, provider positioning, apparent buyer type, expected evidence, and alignment with Worktree's offer.

The quantitative layer uses Google Trends, WordStream's public keyword tool, and Ahrefs' public keyword generator. These sources are useful but not equivalent to first-party Search Console history or an authenticated Google Ads Keyword Planner export. Their values are estimates, use different data and grouping methods, and sometimes disagree materially. They should be treated as supporting evidence and expressed with source attribution rather than blended into false precision.

## Scoring method

Each query is scored from 1 to 5 on five equally visible factors:

1. **Business-model fit:** how closely the searcher's likely need matches hands-on implementation plus ongoing operation for an established mid-market business.
2. **Buyer value:** the likelihood that a suitable engagement can support meaningful implementation and ongoing service value.
3. **Commercial intent:** how strongly the results indicate an active search for a provider rather than software, education, jobs, or general information.
4. **Ranking feasibility:** the relative opportunity visible in the current result set. A higher score means a more attainable result set, not a measured keyword-difficulty score.
5. **Evidence readiness:** Worktree's current ability to support the intent accurately with its offer, operating artifacts, product surfaces, and near-term design-partner evidence.

Scores guide priority but do not mechanically determine page count. Search intent and internal cannibalization decide whether a phrase owns a page, supports another page, or is deferred.

Demand confidence is expressed as `high`, `medium`, or `low` based on agreement among the public estimates, the Trends signal, result density, and commercial specialization. It is not a claim about exact monthly searches.

## Public demand evidence

### Method

- **Google Trends:** exact search terms, Web Search, past 12 months, United States or Canada. Values are normalized relative interest, not monthly searches. To make separate comparisons directionally comparable, `AI automation services` was retained as a common anchor. Google explains that Trends uses a normalized sample of actual searches on a 0–100 scale; a zero can mean insufficient reportable data rather than no searches. See [Google Trends data methodology](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533).
- **WordStream:** public United States keyword results. The tool reports estimated monthly search volume, top-of-page bid ranges, and paid competition using Google keyword research data. See [WordStream's public keyword tool](https://www.wordstream.com/keyword-research-tool).
- **Ahrefs:** public Google keyword generator for the United States and Canada. The tool reports volume bands and Keyword Difficulty where available using Ahrefs' clickstream-supported database. See [Ahrefs' public keyword generator](https://ahrefs.com/keyword-generator).

These figures are a dated snapshot and should be reproduced before a major architecture change.

### Canada and Vancouver evidence

In the Canadian 12-month Trends comparison, `AI automation services` averaged 42 relative points, compared with 9 for `managed AI services`, 9 for `AI automation agency`, 7 for `AI implementation company`, and 2 for `AI implementation services`.

Ahrefs reports Canadian `AI automation services` at `>100` monthly searches and `AI implementation services` at `<100`. This supports retaining shared national pages while weighting the Canadian acquisition language somewhat more toward automation than implementation.

For Vancouver-modified terms searched across Canada, Trends averaged 14 for `AI automation Vancouver`, 1 for `AI automation agency Vancouver`, and 0 for the exact `AI consultant Vancouver`, `AI implementation Vancouver`, and `AI agent development Vancouver` phrases. Ahrefs reports `AI automation Vancouver` at `<100` monthly searches. This is small local demand, but it is the only tested Vancouver phrase with a consistent measurable signal.

### Quantitative correction

The public data changes the initial terminology-led recommendation:

1. `AI implementation services` should own the implementation page. `AI agent implementation services` and `AI agent deployment services` should support it rather than own separate URLs.
2. `AI automation services` should be the automation page's head term. `AI workflow automation services` is the high-intent refinement that keeps the page aligned with recurring operational work.
3. `managed AI services` should carry the searchable head term for the ongoing-service page. `Managed AI operations` remains Worktree's more precise differentiating language.
4. `AI automation Vancouver` should lead the local page's query mapping. Implementation-company language should qualify the offer and buyer, not replace the only local phrase with measurable demand.
5. `AI automation agency` has enough demand to monitor and potentially address through a comparison or buyer-decision page, but its poor intent quality means Worktree should not adopt the agency label as its category.

#### Why these lead

`AI implementation services` has substantially more measurable demand than its agent-specific variant while preserving strong commercial intent. The implementation page should use the broader head term and establish Worktree's scope through agent, workflow, deployment, permissions, human review, testing, launch, runbook, and monitoring language. The narrower result set represented by [Dotnitron](https://www.dotnitron.com/services/ai-agent-implementation), [Olyra](https://olyra.ai/services/ai-agent-implementation), and [HireOverseas](https://www.hireoverseas.com/ai-implementation-agent) remains useful evidence for what agent-implementation buyers expect.

`AI automation services` supplies the broad measurable demand. `AI workflow automation services` supplies the cleaner commercial interpretation: operational examples, integrations, exception handling, implementation scope, proof, and ongoing support. One page should combine them so Worktree can enter the larger market without becoming a generic automation agency. Representative aligned results include [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/services/ai-workflow-automation), [Sennova](https://www.sennovalabs.com/services/ai-workflow-automation), and [Avaib](https://www.avaib.com/ai-solutions/workflow-automation/).

`managed AI services` is the measurable head term, but its result set often means infrastructure, model operations, data pipelines, cloud platforms, or enterprise MLOps. `Managed AI operations` gives Worktree a more precise service definition: monitoring quality, incidents, integrations, model or prompt changes, usage, cost, reporting, and ongoing improvement. Current providers such as [SandRock AI](https://www.sandrock.ai/services/managed-ai-operations), [ProIncident](https://www.proincident.com/managed-ai-services), and [Northern Logic](https://northernlogic.ai/solutions) show how the two phrases overlap. Worktree must distinguish its supported business-hours service from competitors that advertise unsupported 24/7 coverage or availability guarantees.

`AI implementation partner` is commercially valuable but semantically mixed. Results range from credible implementation firms to small-business setup services, training programs, and freelancer-style offers. It is best used as the broad relationship Worktree names on the homepage, while the more precise service pages establish what the partnership includes. It should not receive a duplicative standalone page unless later query data proves a distinct intent.

## United States and Canada interpretation

The shared national-site decision remains correct.

- United States results contain a deeper bench of specialized implementation and managed-service firms. National pages should therefore use clear implementation, deployment, operational-control, and ongoing-service language capable of competing in that market.
- Canadian results are thinner and more frequently oriented toward small and medium businesses, general automation, custom development, and local consulting. Examples include [Docscope](https://docscope.ai/services/ai-automation), [Agentika](https://agentika.ca/), and [Vatsal Shah](https://vatsalshah.ca/services/workflow-automation).
- The difference is not currently large enough to justify duplicating every commercial page. The shared pages should state United States and Canadian availability and include Canadian contracting, privacy, and data-handling facts when they are verified and relevant.
- The Vancouver page and Business Profile provide the distinct local surface. A broader Canadian clone would divide authority without yet serving a materially different buyer job.

## What ranking pages must prove

Across the priority result sets, a commercial page is unlikely to convert a mid-market buyer with category copy alone. The initial pages should make the following evidence visible as it becomes real:

1. A concrete recurring workflow and its before-state.
2. The systems and information the workflow touches.
3. The implementation sequence from mapping through launch.
4. Access boundaries and the point at which a person reviews or approves work.
5. Test cases, quality checks, exception handling, and a recoverable operating path.
6. What Worktree monitors and changes after launch.
7. A defined business baseline and an honest way to measure improvement.
8. A real customer result, review, or case study once permission exists.

Until customer evidence exists, product demonstrations, deployment artifacts, process clarity, and precise boundaries can establish competence. They must not be presented as client proof or unsupported service-level commitments.

## Implications for the URL map

Phase 3 should begin from five page jobs, not from every phrase in the tables:

1. **Homepage/category:** Worktree as the AI implementation partner that gets a valuable workflow into operation and remains accountable for it.
2. **Implementation:** own `AI implementation services`, supported by agent implementation, deployment, company, and partner variants.
3. **Workflow automation:** own `AI automation services`, qualified by `AI workflow automation services` and recognizable operational language.
4. **Managed operation:** own `managed AI services`, using `managed AI operations` to define the actual ongoing service and avoid infrastructure ambiguity.
5. **Local implementation:** own `AI automation Vancouver`, supported by Vancouver implementation, consulting, agency, and development variants without adopting their weaker category implications.

These are page jobs, not approved URLs. The next phase must inspect the live and repository routes before deciding whether to retain, reposition, consolidate, or create pages.

## Measurement plan

Once measurement access exists, append the following for every Tier 1 and local query cluster:

- Google Ads Keyword Planner monthly range for the United States, Canada, and Vancouver where available.
- Search Console impressions, clicks, average position, page, and country after indexing.
- A reputable third-party difficulty and parent-topic estimate, recorded with its provider and date.
- Qualified inquiry count and fit by landing page and query where privacy thresholds permit.
- Queries Google associates with each page, used to detect intent drift and cannibalization.

The first useful decision point is not an arbitrary traffic threshold. It is whether impressions and inquiries confirm that the page attracts implementation buyers rather than software shoppers, low-budget automation requests, or strategy-only consulting demand.
