# Worktree SEO market strategy

Status: Approved strategy  
Last updated: 2026-08-20

## Purpose

This document records the market and search territory Worktree intends to own. It is the governing strategy for later keyword research, page architecture, copy briefs, technical SEO, local SEO, evidence collection, and authority building.

It does not define the final URL map or content backlog. Those should be derived from this strategy rather than treated as the strategy itself.

## Desired outcome

Worktree should become a prominent search result for businesses looking for a partner to put AI agents and workflows into production and keep them operating.

The initial SEO buyer is an established mid-market company with recurring operational work, multiple business systems, identifiable process owners, and the ability to fund hands-on implementation and ongoing operation. Exact employee and revenue thresholds are not fixed by this document; operational maturity and engagement fit are more important than a nominal company-size boundary.

The site should attract buyers seeking hands-on implementation and ongoing accountability. It should not optimize toward buyers primarily looking for a self-serve SaaS tool, generic strategy consulting, staff augmentation, or inexpensive no-code automation.

## Category position

Worktree is an AI implementation and operating partner.

The core offer is a high-touch service that maps a real workflow, connects the required systems, defines access and human approvals, tests the routine, launches it, and remains involved in its operation and improvement. Product surfaces and platform capabilities substantiate that service; they are not the primary category being sold.

## Geographic model

Worktree will use two complementary acquisition tracks.

### National organic search

- Target buyers across the United States and Canada from one shared, authoritative website.
- Weight commercial language and authority-building toward the United States, where Worktree prefers to acquire clients.
- State clearly that Worktree serves businesses across both the United States and Canada.
- Keep one canonical page for each national search intent so backlinks, content depth, engagement, and authority accumulate on one URL.
- Track United States and Canadian rankings, impressions, traffic, and conversions separately.

### Lower Mainland local search

- Capture local demand through a legitimate Google Business Profile based at Worktree's real operating location in the Lower Mainland.
- Configure the profile as a service-area business and hide the residential address if customers are not received there.
- Use only accurate service areas that Worktree can genuinely serve in person.
- Support the profile with one substantive Vancouver/Lower Mainland commercial landing page.
- Build local authority through genuine reviews, client evidence, citations, partnerships, and relevant local links.
- Add municipality-specific pages only when real clients, relationships, evidence, or materially distinct information justify them. Do not create thin doorway pages for every city.

The local track complements national organic search. It does not replace it and does not define the national brand.

## Localization decision

Worktree will not launch a complete US/Canada localization system initially.

The initial model is:

- One shared national page for each important commercial intent.
- US-weighted commercial presentation with explicit US and Canadian availability.
- A dedicated Lower Mainland local surface for Maps and local organic demand.
- Canadian company, contracting, privacy, and data-handling facts available wherever they materially affect buyer trust.

Regional URL variants should be introduced only when search and conversion data show that a shared page is failing to serve a material regional opportunity, or when currency, regulation, contracting, data residency, proof, or buyer questions make the regional content meaningfully different.

If regional variants are introduced later, they must use stable URLs, reciprocal `hreflang` annotations, accurate regional signals, and visible locale selection. Worktree should not use automatic IP-based redirects.

## Search territory

### Primary commercial queries

These queries most directly describe the buyer and engagement Worktree wants:

1. `AI implementation services`
2. `AI automation services`
3. `managed AI services`
4. `AI implementation partner`

### High-fit commercial refinements

These queries sharpen the page-owning commercial clusters but do not initially require independent URLs:

1. `AI agent implementation services`
2. `AI agent deployment services`
3. `AI workflow automation services`
4. `managed AI operations`
5. `AI implementation company`

### Supporting acquisition queries

These queries may provide broader demand, but must not pull Worktree toward generic, low-trust automation-agency positioning:

1. `AI integration services`
2. `business automation services`
3. `business workflow automation`
4. `agentic AI services`
5. `AI automation agency`

`AI integration services` is the highest-priority adjacent commercial query for Phase 3. It should be tested for intent overlap with the implementation page before Worktree creates another URL.

### Buyer-decision and authority queries

The initial authority program should evaluate:

1. `AI automation cost`
2. `AI implementation cost`
3. `AI automation ROI`
4. `AI agent security`
5. `AI agent evaluation`
6. `AI agent governance`
7. `AI agent monitoring`
8. `AI agent maintenance`

These queries support commercial conversion, buyer confidence, and topical authority. They do not authorize unsupported pricing, ROI, security, service-level, or operating claims.

### Local queries

The Lower Mainland effort should prioritize and evaluate relevant variations such as:

1. `AI automation Vancouver`
2. `AI services Vancouver`
3. `AI consulting Vancouver`
4. `AI implementation company Vancouver`
5. `AI automation agency Vancouver`
6. `AI agent development Vancouver`
7. Relevant `near me` searches surfaced through the Google Business Profile

The local page should own `AI automation Vancouver`. The remaining variants are supporting language unless later search and conversion data establishes a distinct buyer intent.

### Queries that do not define the strategy

- `AI consulting company`: too broad and too strongly associated with strategy-only engagements.
- `AI agent development company`: useful as a supporting term, but commonly associated with outsourced project development rather than ongoing operation.
- `AI transformation`: vague, enterprise-oriented, and poorly aligned with Worktree's focused first-workflow model.
- `AI automation tools`: primarily software and platform research rather than implementation-partner intent.
- `AI agents for business`: mixed informational and product intent; useful for education, not as the primary commercial category.
- `managed AI workflows`: little reliable measured demand and substantial managed-Airflow and workflow-software contamination.
- `AI agent management services`: current intent favors software, governance platforms, and control planes.
- `generative AI implementation services`: below reliable public demand reporting in the ratified comparison.

## Ranking objective

The working 12-to-18-month objective is:

> Make Worktree a prominent United States result for AI implementation services, AI automation services, and managed AI services; use agent implementation, workflow automation, and managed operations to establish a precise point of view; and preserve a credible Canadian and Lower Mainland acquisition surface.

Success is not ranking alone. The program must produce qualified deployment-review inquiries from established mid-market businesses that fit Worktree's implementation and operating model.

## Strategic principles

1. Choose search territory before pages. URLs, navigation, copy, content, and schema follow the intended query and buyer.
2. One page should own one search intent. Avoid internal competition between pages targeting the same query cluster.
3. Evidence is the authority engine. Real deployments, operating artifacts, measured baselines, run histories, case studies, and customer reviews are more valuable than generic publishing volume.
4. Build depth before breadth. Worktree should not imitate competitors that manufacture dozens of lightly differentiated industry pages.
5. Localize only when the market difference is real. Currency swaps and place-name insertion are not sufficient reasons to duplicate a page.
6. Product proof supports the service sale. SEO copy must not reposition Worktree as a self-serve SaaS platform.
7. National and local search are separate systems. National pages pursue category authority; the Business Profile and local landing page pursue geographic relevance.
8. Preserve accuracy. Do not invent client proof, integrations, outcomes, service levels, security capabilities, geographic presence, or regulatory support.

## Initial execution sequence

1. Correct the live technical SEO and deployment mismatch: working `robots.txt`, sitemap, canonical metadata, social metadata, structured data, redirects, indexable commercial routes, and expected 404 behavior.
2. Establish measurement: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, sitemap submission, indexed-page baseline, country-segmented query visibility, and conversion attribution.
3. Produce the keyword-to-page map: assign national and local query clusters to existing or proposed URLs and resolve cannibalization before drafting copy.
4. Build the national commercial core around the primary and differentiated query clusters.
5. Create the legitimate Lower Mainland Google Business Profile and its supporting local page.
6. Build authority through deployment evidence, customer reviews, case studies, relevant directories, partnerships, and earned links.
7. Revisit regional localization only after enough search and conversion data exists to justify the added page and maintenance surface.

## Google Business Profile guardrails

- Worktree must make genuine in-person contact with customers to be eligible.
- Use the real operating location for verification.
- Hide the address when customers are not served there.
- Do not use a mailbox, virtual office, or unstaffed coworking address.
- Do not create a false American location.
- Use the real public-facing business name without geographic or keyword stuffing.
- Maintain one profile for the real local operation unless Worktree later establishes a separate, staffed, customer-facing location with its own personnel.

## Dependencies and decisions still required

The following are intentionally not decided by this document:

- The practical qualification signals that distinguish a suitable mid-market buyer; exact employee and revenue thresholds remain intentionally open.
- Authenticated Google Ads, paid SEO-platform, and Search Console data that can refine or supersede the approved public research baseline.
- The exact keyword-to-URL map and whether existing product routes or new service routes should carry each commercial intent.
- The final URL and wording for the Vancouver/Lower Mainland landing page.
- The Google Business Profile categories and exact service areas.
- The first workflow and design-partner evidence that will anchor the first use-case and case-study cluster.

These decisions should be made with current search-result research, actual operating facts, and the first deployment evidence.
