### Section: workflow anatomy

**Eyebrow:** Start with the real work

## Give the system a job your team can explain.

The strongest starting point is recurring work your team already understands: what triggers it, which context matters, what usually happens next, and where the difficult decisions appear. Worktree maps that routine with the people who perform and own it, then uses the map to shape the implementation.

**Workflow stages:**

1. **Signal**  
   A request, schedule, message, or system event begins the work.

2. **Context**  
   The workflow gathers the selected information needed for this case.

3. **Routine**  
   The agent follows the agreed instructions and prepares or performs the next step.

4. **Judgment**  
   Approval, ambiguity, and exceptions reach the right person.

5. **Record**  
   The relevant output and operating evidence remain available for review.

### Section: implementation path

<a id="implementation-path"></a>

**Eyebrow:** One path to production

## Move from the current process to a controlled launch.

Worktree handles the implementation as one connected responsibility. Strategy, integration, control design, testing, and launch are not separate recommendations for your team to coordinate afterward.

#### 01 — Understand the work

We work with the process owner and the people closest to the routine. Together, we identify the objective, current steps, source information, handoffs, exceptions, risks, and the operational change worth making.

#### 02 — Define success and authority

We turn the desired result into representative cases and acceptance criteria. The team decides which actions may proceed, which should remain draft-only, and where approval belongs.

#### 03 — Design the workflow

We define the trigger, required context, instructions, selected systems, actions, escalation paths, expected output, and evidence needed to understand what happened.

#### 04 — Connect and build

Worktree configures the workflow around the agreed tools, permissions, and operating boundaries. Integration decisions follow the job rather than forcing the process into a standard connector catalogue.

#### 05 — Test the usual and difficult cases

We evaluate representative work, missing context, exceptions, and failure conditions against the agreed standard. Results inform focused changes before acceptance.

#### 06 — Accept and launch

The team reviews the working instructions, controls, evaluation criteria, known limitations, and launch plan. Accepted decisions begin the Deployment Record and the transition into operation.

### Section: integration and control

**Eyebrow:** The workflow inside your operation

## Connect the work, not just the software.

AI integration services are useful when they connect a real business routine—not when they merely prove that two tools can exchange data. Worktree designs the complete path around the selected systems and the responsibility the workflow is meant to carry.

**The implementation accounts for:**

- **Triggers:** What starts the work and how duplicate or incomplete requests are handled.
- **Context:** Which sources may be consulted and which information should be treated as authoritative.
- **Access:** What the workflow can read, prepare, change, or never touch.
- **Approvals:** Which actions require a person to review or decide.
- **Exceptions:** Where missing context, ambiguity, and failures should go.
- **Outputs:** What is created, where it belongs, and how the team knows the routine finished.

The right controls depend on the workflow, systems, and intended actions. Worktree confirms those requirements with your team before deployment.

### Section: frequently asked questions

**Eyebrow:** Before the review

## Common implementation questions.

#### What do AI implementation services include?

Worktree's implementation covers understanding the current workflow, defining success and authority, designing the operating path, connecting selected systems, configuring the agent workflow, testing representative and failure cases, supporting acceptance, and launching into managed operation.

#### Do we need a complete technical specification first?

No. Bring the workflow as it runs today, the people and systems it touches, recent examples, and the change you want. Worktree uses the review and consultation to make the operating and technical requirements explicit before build.

#### Can Worktree integrate with our existing systems?

The deployment review identifies the systems, access, actions, data boundaries, and technical dependencies involved. Worktree confirms feasibility and the appropriate integration approach for the selected workflow before committing to scope.

#### Where do human approvals fit?

Your team retains the business authority. Worktree helps identify the points where an action may proceed, remain draft-only, or require a person to review and decide. The appropriate design depends on the workflow and its consequences.

#### What happens after launch?

Worktree can continue operating, evaluating, supporting, and improving the deployed workflow under the agreed service scope. Material changes, limitations, risks, and next actions are reflected in the Deployment Record.

#### How is pricing determined?

The deployment review clarifies the workflow, systems, permissions, evaluation requirements, operating support, and other dependencies. Worktree uses that understanding to define the scope and commercial proposal.
