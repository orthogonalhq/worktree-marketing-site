# Worktree homepage narrative strategy

Status: Locked direction  
Locked: 2026-08-20

## Purpose

This document defines the job, narrative sequence, and scope of the Worktree homepage. It governs future homepage copy and section design unless a later approved strategy explicitly replaces it.

The homepage should be short, visually varied, and easy to understand. It should help a business recognize where Worktree could help, establish enough confidence to continue, and make talking to a Worktree engineer feel like the natural next step.

It should not attempt to explain every product capability, implementation detail, security mechanism, operating artifact, or comparison on one page.

## Desired outcome

The homepage should leave a qualified visitor with six conclusions:

1. Worktree combines secure agent infrastructure with engineers who work alongside customer teams.
2. AI agents can create useful capacity in recognizable parts of their business.
3. Worktree has an informed and active point of view about putting agents into operation.
4. The offer rests on tangible infrastructure and managed operating capability, not demonstrations alone.
5. Businesses like theirs can work with Worktree and achieve a meaningful operational change.
6. They can begin with a conversation about where work is getting stuck.

## Homepage and Product separation

The homepage helps visitors qualify themselves and become inspired by what Worktree can help them accomplish.

The Product narrative helps those visitors qualify Worktree's product and operating model for the opportunity they now recognize. It should explain the shared infrastructure, deployment system, controls, evidence, managed operations, and routes into deeper product pages.

The homepage can introduce those ideas, but Product owns their detailed explanation.

## Final page structure

1. Hero
2. Workflow gallery
3. Featured blog post or field note
4. Infrastructure and Product credibility
5. Customer stories and case study
6. Final CTA

## Section requirements

### 1. Hero

The hero defines the category and the relationship in plain language. It should communicate both sides of the offer:

- Secure agent infrastructure.
- Engineers who work alongside the customer's team.

It should establish ambition without attempting to summarize the entire delivery model. The primary CTA is a conversation with a Worktree engineer. The secondary CTA leads to the main Product narrative.

### 2. Workflow gallery

The gallery is the primary inspiration and self-qualification chapter. It should show specific work a visitor can recognize, not abstract workflow categories.

Each card should be understandable without opening it. Expansions can provide more detail, but they must not all rely on the same visual or narrative template. The form of each expansion should follow the character of its story.

Security may appear as one distinct card because safe access is itself a meaningful business capability. It should not absorb or be conflated with the clinical workflow story.

### 3. Featured insight

This is one editorial spotlight, not a generic news carousel. By default, it features the latest published blog post. Worktree can manually promote a more important field note, research article, deployment observation, or major update when one deserves priority.

Its job is to demonstrate momentum, taste, and category knowledge while giving the page a visual and narrative interruption after the gallery. Until more writing exists, the current post occupies this chapter by default; no multi-item carousel or artificial content inventory is required.

### 4. Infrastructure and Product credibility

This chapter follows possibility with reasons to believe. It should show that Worktree's deployments rest on a real operating foundation.

The chapter may introduce:

- Controlled access and human approval boundaries.
- Defined agent execution environments.
- Representative tests and evaluations.
- Reviewable work and operating evidence.
- Ongoing management and improvement after launch.

It should remain concise and link to the main Product narrative for the complete explanation. It must not become a miniature technical documentation page.

### 5. Customer stories and case study

This chapter combines the jobs Stripe distributes across customer segments, professional services, and testimonials.

The intended proof mix is:

- One strong video testimonial or customer conversation.
- A small number of concise written testimonials.
- One substantive case study showing the situation, the work, the human relationship, and the operational change.

The embedded Worktree engineer relationship should become tangible through these stories instead of requiring a separate generic process section.

Only real, permissioned customer evidence may be used. If the required evidence is not yet available, the section should remain smaller or unpublished rather than being filled with implied or fabricated proof.

### 6. Final CTA

The final CTA should be direct and conversational. By this point, the visitor should understand the possibility, the operating foundation, and the nature of the relationship.

The CTA should invite them to bring a recurring process, bottleneck, or area where they need more capacity and talk to a Worktree engineer.

## Material removed from the homepage narrative

The following subjects are no longer independent homepage chapters:

- A second generic workflow-example section.
- A standalone explanation of the Worktree relationship.
- A standalone "start with one job" process section.
- A standalone portfolio-growth section.
- A detailed deliverables ledger.
- A second access-and-control explanation after the security card.
- The AI approach comparison.
- A separate news carousel or cultural-content section.
- A standalone testimonial section disconnected from customer stories.

Useful copy from these sections is preserved in `docs/homepage-copy-archive-2026-08-20.mdx`. It can be adapted for Product, managed operations, implementation, security, use-case, comparison, conversion, or case-study pages.

## Visual direction

The six chapters should not share one repeated component grammar. Each should perform a different visual role:

1. **Hero:** expressive and category-defining.
2. **Gallery:** dense, exploratory, and varied.
3. **Featured insight:** editorial and interruptive.
4. **Infrastructure:** restrained, credible, and technically grounded.
5. **Customer stories:** human, documentary, and evidence-led.
6. **Final CTA:** simple and decisive.

The page should feel intentionally paced rather than uniformly card-based. Section transitions, scale changes, background shifts, typography, and media should make the change in narrative task perceptible.

## Copy and evidence guardrails

- Lead with the customer's situation and desired improvement.
- Explain internal mechanisms only after their relevance is clear.
- Keep the embedded engineer and ongoing operation central to the offer.
- Treat one workflow as the practical starting point, not the limit of the relationship.
- Avoid generic AI transformation language, internal platform jargon, and unexplained acronyms.
- Do not invent customer proof, metrics, integrations, service levels, runtime isolation, security guarantees, or operating outcomes.
- Clearly distinguish illustrative product demonstrations from customer evidence.
- Use Product and deeper pages to elaborate rather than forcing every detail into the homepage.

## Implementation principle

Build and review one chapter at a time. A chapter is complete only when its copy, visual form, responsive behavior, and exit into the next page or action all perform the intended narrative task.

Removing the superseded homepage sections should happen only after their replacement chapters have been approved and their reusable copy has been routed to the appropriate future pages.
